If your mattress feels too firm when you lie on your side — like your shoulder and hip are pressing into a hard surface — a mattress topper can be an affordable way to add cushioning without replacing the whole bed.

But toppers aren't magic. They can change how the surface *feels*, but they can't fix a mattress that's sagging or worn out. This guide explains how toppers work for side sleepers, how to choose the right thickness, material and firmness, and how to tell whether a topper or a new mattress is the better move.

Our guidance is research-based, built from how topper materials generally behave, manufacturer information and recurring buyer feedback — not hands-on testing.

## Why side sleepers often want a softer surface

When you sleep on your side, your weight concentrates on a smaller area — mainly your **shoulder and hip**. On a very firm surface, those points can't sink in, which can push your spine out of a straight line and feel uncomfortable.

A surface with some "give" lets your shoulder and hip settle in slightly while the rest of the mattress supports your waist. That's why many side sleepers prefer a **medium-soft to medium** feel on top — and why a topper can be such a useful upgrade on a firm bed.

## Topper vs. mattress pad vs. new mattress

These terms get mixed up a lot:

- **Mattress pad:** a thin quilted layer, mostly for protection and a touch of softness.
- **Mattress topper:** a thicker comfort layer (usually 1–4 inches) that meaningfully changes how the bed feels.
- **New mattress:** replaces the whole support system and comfort layers.

### When a topper may make sense

- Your mattress is **in good shape** (no sagging or deep body impressions) but feels **too firm**.
- You want to **soften a guest bed**, dorm bed or newer mattress that didn't turn out as expected.
- You're on a **budget** or not ready to replace your mattress yet.
- You and a partner want to tweak the feel — some toppers come in split versions.

### When a new mattress may make more sense

- There's **visible sagging**, lumps or deep body impressions.
- The mattress is **old or worn out** and no longer feels supportive.
- You feel **springs or coils** through the surface.
- It's **too soft** already — a topper generally can't make a sagging mattress firmer in a meaningful way.

A topper laid on top of a dip will usually just sink into the dip. If that's your situation, a topper is unlikely to deliver the comfort you're hoping for.

## Step 1: Choose the right thickness

Thickness is the biggest factor in how much a topper changes your bed.

| Thickness | What it tends to do | May suit side sleepers who… |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 inches | Adds a light cushion | Have a medium mattress and want a little more softness |
| 3 inches | Noticeable cushioning for shoulders and hips | Have a firm mattress — a popular middle ground |
| 4 inches | Deep, plush feel | Have a very firm mattress or prefer a strong sink-in feel |

Two things to keep in mind:

- **Thicker toppers raise your bed height** and may need deeper-pocket fitted sheets.
- **Thicker toppers can change your ideal pillow height.** As your shoulder sinks deeper, you may need a slightly lower pillow. Our [pillow buying guide](/guides/how-to-choose-a-pillow-for-side-sleeping/) explains how to check.

## Step 2: Compare topper materials

### Memory foam

Memory foam toppers contour slowly to your body, creating a cradling feel around the shoulder and hip. They're widely available and come in many densities and price points.

- **Pros:** strong contouring, good motion isolation, lots of options.
- **Cons:** can feel warm, slow response can make turning over feel harder, possible new-foam odor at first.
- **Look for:** foam density information, ventilation or open-cell design, and a removable cover if possible.

### Latex

Latex toppers add cushioning with a buoyant, responsive feel. You sink in a little, but the surface pushes back, making it easier to change positions. They come in Dunlop (often denser) and Talalay (often softer and lighter) varieties, and in natural, synthetic or blended latex. - **Pros:** responsive, generally breathable, often durable.
- **Cons:** heavier, usually more expensive, not suitable for people with latex allergies.
- **Look for:** clear latex type and processing method, firmness options (soft or medium are common choices for side sleepers) and certifications listed by the manufacturer.

### Fiber, down and down-alternative (featherbeds)

These toppers are filled with polyester fiber, down, feathers or a blend. They feel soft and pillowy rather than contouring.

- **Pros:** plush, cozy surface, often lighter, some are machine-washable.
- **Cons:** less pressure relief than foam or latex, fill can shift and flatten, need regular shaking out.
- **Look for:** baffle-box or channel stitching to keep fill in place, a secure strap or skirt system and washability.

### Other options

You may also see wool toppers (often chosen for temperature regulation and a gentle cushion) and polyfoam toppers (usually budget-friendly, but sometimes less durable). Hybrid toppers combine layers, such as foam with a fiber cover.

## Step 3: Pick the right firmness

For most side sleepers on a firm mattress, a **soft to medium** topper is the usual choice. A topper that's too firm won't add the cushioning you want; one that's too soft and thick may let your hips sink too far.

Body size matters, too. Heavier people tend to compress a topper more, so they may want a slightly thicker or firmer option to avoid "bottoming out." Lighter people may find a thinner, softer topper is plenty.

## Step 4: Think about temperature

Toppers sit right under your body, so they can affect how warm you sleep.

- Solid memory foam generally retains the most heat.
- Latex, wool and ventilated foams are often described as more breathable.
- A breathable mattress protector and natural-fiber sheets can help too.

If heat is a recurring issue, our guide on [making your bedroom more comfortable for sleep](/guides/how-to-make-your-bedroom-more-comfortable-for-sleep/) has more ideas.

## Step 5: Check fit, care and policies

Before you buy, check:

- **Size:** match your mattress size exactly (twin, full, queen, king, etc.).
- **Staying in place:** corner straps, a fitted skirt or a non-slip base help prevent sliding.
- **Cover:** a removable, washable cover is a big plus.
- **Setup:** foam toppers often arrive compressed and need time to expand and air out.
- **Trial period and warranty:** comfort is personal, so look for a fair return policy.

## Frequently asked questions

### What thickness topper is best for side sleepers?

Many side sleepers on a firm mattress look at **3-inch** toppers as a balance of cushioning and support. A 2-inch topper may be enough on a medium mattress, while a 4-inch topper suits those who want a plush, deep feel.

### Can a mattress topper fix a sagging mattress?

Generally no. A topper adds comfort to the surface but follows the shape of whatever is underneath. If your mattress is sagging or worn out, replacing it is usually the better long-term option.

### Memory foam or latex topper — which is better?

Neither is better for everyone. Memory foam offers a deep, contouring feel; latex offers responsive, buoyant cushioning and often sleeps cooler. Choose based on feel, temperature and budget — and skip latex if you have a latex allergy.

### Will a topper help with shoulder or hip pain?

We can't make medical claims. Many side sleepers simply find a softer surface more comfortable. If you have ongoing pain, please talk with a healthcare professional.

### Do I need to change my pillow after adding a topper?

Possibly. A thick, soft topper lets your shoulder sink deeper, which can reduce the gap your pillow needs to fill. Re-check your head and neck alignment after a week or so.
