There is no single "best pillow" for every side sleeper. The pillow that feels perfect to one person can feel too tall, too firm or too warm to the next. What *does* exist is a best pillow **type** for your body, your mattress and your preferences — and once you know the type, choosing a specific product gets much easier.

This guide compares the five pillow types side sleepers most often consider. For each one, we explain how it feels, who it may suit, its potential drawbacks and what to look for when you shop. Below each type you'll find a recommendation slot where we highlight a product that fits the criteria.

> **How we put this together:** This comparison is research-based. It draws on how each material generally performs, manufacturer specifications and care information, and recurring themes in buyer feedback. We have not hands-on tested these products, and we don't publish made-up ratings or test scores.

## What to look for in any side-sleeper pillow

Before we get into the types, here are the criteria we used to judge every category:

- **Enough loft** to keep the head level with the spine when lying on your side.
- **Shape retention** through the night — the pillow shouldn't collapse under your head.
- **A supportive edge** that fills the space under the neck, not just the head.
- **Breathable, washable cover** — side sleepers have lots of face contact with the pillow.
- **Clear trial period, return policy and warranty.**
- **Honest material information**, including any certifications the manufacturer lists.

## 1. Adjustable shredded foam pillows

Shredded foam pillows are filled with small pieces of memory foam (sometimes blended with fiber). The cover usually zips open so you can remove or add fill until the height feels right.

### Who it may suit

- Side sleepers who aren't sure what loft they need.
- People who switch between side and back sleeping and want to fine-tune.
- Anyone who likes a pillow they can scrunch and shape around their neck.

### Potential drawbacks

- Some people notice a lumpy or "beanbag" feel.
- The fill may shift during the night and need fluffing in the morning.
- Removing fill can be a little messy; keep the extra in a bag for later.
- New foam pillows may have a temporary odor when first unpacked.

### What to look for

A zippered inner liner (so fill doesn't escape), extra fill included, a breathable outer cover and clear washing instructions.

## 2. Solid memory foam pillows

A solid memory foam pillow is a single block of foam, often in a classic rectangle or a loaf shape. It softens slightly with body heat and slowly contours to your head and neck.

### Who it may suit

- Side sleepers who want the same height and support every night.
- People who dislike the shifting feel of loose fill.
- Those who enjoy a "hugged" or cradled sensation.

### Potential drawbacks

- Loft is fixed, so you need to choose the right height up front.
- Foam can hold heat, which some warm sleepers notice.
- The slow response can feel "stuck" when you change positions.
- The inner foam usually can't be machine-washed.

### What to look for

A loft that matches your shoulder width (some brands offer multiple heights), ventilation holes or open-cell foam, a removable washable cover and information about foam certifications.

## 3. Latex pillows

Latex pillows are made from natural, synthetic or blended latex foam, either as a solid block or as shredded pieces. Latex is known for its springy, responsive feel — it supports your head without letting it sink deeply.

### Who it may suit

- Side sleepers who want firm, buoyant support.
- People who change positions often and like a pillow that responds quickly.
- Warm sleepers looking for a typically more breathable option than solid memory foam.

### Potential drawbacks

- Latex pillows are often heavier and can cost more.
- Some people find the bounce too lively.
- **Anyone with a latex allergy should avoid latex products** or check with a healthcare professional first.
- Solid latex pillows have a fixed loft.

### What to look for

Clear information on whether the latex is natural, synthetic or blended, the latex processing type (Dunlop or Talalay), available loft options and a breathable cover. For a deeper comparison, read [Memory Foam vs. Latex Pillows](/guides/memory-foam-vs-latex-pillows/).

## 4. Down and down-alternative pillows

Down pillows are filled with the soft under-feathers of ducks or geese (sometimes blended with feathers). Down-alternative pillows use synthetic fibers designed to mimic that light, fluffy feel.

### Who it may suit

- Side sleepers who love a soft, plush, "hotel-style" pillow.
- People who like to scrunch and reshape their pillow.
- Those who want a lightweight pillow that's easy to move around.

### Potential drawbacks

- Many down pillows compress quite a bit — side sleepers often need a **firm or extra-fill** version to keep enough height.
- They need regular fluffing to restore loft.
- Natural down may not suit people with sensitivities to feathers; down alternative is an option.
- Lower-quality fibers can clump over time.

### What to look for

A firmness or fill level labeled for side sleepers, information about fill power and responsible sourcing (for real down), a tightly woven cover to keep fill in and care instructions — many down-alternative pillows can be machine-washed.

## 5. Contoured (cervical-shaped) pillows

Contoured pillows have a sculpted shape — often a higher edge for the neck and a lower dip for the head, or a cut-out area for the shoulder. They're usually made from memory foam or latex.

### Who it may suit

- Side sleepers who like a defined, structured shape under the neck.
- People who find their head rolls off a standard pillow.
- Those who sleep in a consistent position for most of the night.

### Potential drawbacks

- The shape can take a week or two to get used to — and some people never do.
- Contours may feel awkward if you change positions often.
- Fewer size and loft options than standard pillows.
- Contoured pillows are sometimes marketed with health claims — be skeptical of any product that promises to treat pain.

### What to look for

Two different edge heights (so you can flip it to adjust loft), a size that fits your pillowcase, a removable washable cover and a reasonable trial period.

## How to narrow down your choice

Still undecided? Use these quick rules of thumb:

1. **Unsure about height?** Start with an **adjustable shredded foam** pillow.
2. **Want consistency?** Choose **solid memory foam** in a loft that matches your shoulders.
3. **Sleep warm or like bounce?** Look at **latex** (if you don't have a latex sensitivity).
4. **Love a soft, classic feel?** Pick a **firm-fill down or down-alternative** pillow.
5. **Want a structured neck cradle?** Try a **contoured** pillow with two edge heights.

Remember that your mattress changes how a pillow feels. If your bed is very firm, you may want a slightly taller pillow — or consider adding a topper. Our [mattress topper buying guide](/guides/mattress-toppers-for-side-sleepers/) explains how.

## Frequently asked questions

### What type of pillow do most side sleepers prefer?

Preferences vary, but side sleepers commonly look for medium-firm to firm pillows with medium-high to high loft. Adjustable shredded foam pillows are a popular starting point because you can change the height.

### Are expensive pillows worth it?

Not always. A higher price can reflect materials, certifications or warranty length, but comfort is personal. A mid-range pillow with the right loft can feel better than a premium pillow with the wrong one. Prioritize fit and a good trial period.

### How long does it take to adjust to a new pillow?

Many people need several nights to a couple of weeks to get used to a new pillow, especially a firmer or contoured design. If it still feels wrong after that, use the return policy.
