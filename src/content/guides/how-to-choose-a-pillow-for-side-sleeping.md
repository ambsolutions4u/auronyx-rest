If you sleep on your side, your pillow has a bigger job than most people realize. It has to fill the space between your ear and the mattress — a space created by the width of your shoulder — so your head and neck can rest in a relaxed, roughly straight line with your spine.

Get that right and your pillow simply disappears: you stop noticing it. Get it wrong and you may find yourself folding it in half, stacking a second pillow, or tucking your arm underneath to prop up your head.

This guide walks you through every decision that matters, in the order that matters: **loft, firmness, material, shoulder width, adjustability, care and budget**. Our recommendations are research-based — drawn from how materials generally behave, manufacturer information and recurring buyer feedback — not from hands-on lab testing.

## Quick answer: what most side sleepers look for

If you only have a minute, here's the short version:

- **Loft (height):** usually medium-high to high, so your head doesn't tip down toward the mattress.
- **Firmness:** usually medium-firm to firm, so the pillow keeps its height through the night.
- **Shape:** a full, even edge (or a contoured shape) that supports the neck as well as the head.
- **Adjustability:** a removable fill or insert lets you fine-tune height — very helpful if you're unsure.
- **Trial period:** comfort is personal, so a generous return window is worth a lot.

Now let's look at each factor in detail.

## Step 1: Understand loft — the most important factor

**Loft** is how tall the pillow is when your head is resting on it — not how tall it looks on the bed. A fluffy pillow that measures six inches in the store may compress to half that under your head.

For side sleepers, the goal is a loft that matches the distance from the outside of your shoulder to the side of your head. Many bedding guides suggest side sleepers often land somewhere in the medium-high range (roughly four to six inches of *compressed* height), but this varies a lot from person to person.

### Signs your pillow loft is too low

- Your head tilts down toward the mattress.
- You slide a hand or arm under the pillow to raise it.
- You fold the pillow in half or add a second one.
- You wake up feeling like your neck was bent toward the bed.

### Signs your pillow loft is too high

- Your head feels pushed upward, toward the ceiling.
- Your bottom shoulder feels crowded or jammed.
- You push the pillow away or only rest on its edge.

### A simple at-home check

Lie on your side in your normal sleeping position and ask a partner to look at you from behind (or take a photo with a timer). Your nose, chin and the center of your chest should form a fairly straight line with your spine. If your head dips down, you need more loft; if it angles up, you need less.

## Step 2: Choose the right firmness

Firmness and loft work together. A soft pillow may have plenty of loft on the shelf but flatten as soon as your head sinks in. A firmer pillow holds its shape, which is why side sleepers often prefer **medium-firm to firm** pillows.

That said, "firm" doesn't have to mean hard. Some materials, like memory foam and latex, feel cushioned on the surface while still resisting compression underneath. Others, like down, feel soft and moldable but need more fill to stay lofty.

Ask yourself:

- **Do you like to sink in or rest on top?** Sinking in feels cozy but can reduce loft; resting on top keeps alignment more consistent.
- **Do you move around?** Combination sleepers who switch between side and back may prefer a medium feel that works in both positions.

## Step 3: Match the pillow to your shoulder width and body size

This is the part most buying guides skip. Because loft is all about filling the gap above your shoulder, **broader shoulders usually need a taller pillow**, and narrower shoulders usually need a lower one.

Your mattress matters too. On a softer mattress, your shoulder sinks in more, which reduces the gap — so you may need *less* loft. On a firm mattress, your shoulder stays higher, so you may need *more*. If you're also thinking about a topper, read our [mattress topper buying guide for side sleepers](/guides/mattress-toppers-for-side-sleepers/) — a topper can change the pillow height that feels best.

| Your situation | Loft that often works |
|---|---|
| Narrow shoulders, softer mattress | Medium |
| Average shoulders, medium mattress | Medium-high |
| Broad shoulders, firm mattress | High |
| Unsure / sharing pillow preferences | Adjustable |

Treat this table as a starting point, not a rule.

## Step 4: Compare pillow materials

Material affects how a pillow feels, how it holds its shape, how warm it sleeps and how long it lasts. Here's an overview — we cover each type in more depth in [Best Pillows for Side Sleepers: What to Consider](/guides/best-pillows-for-side-sleepers/).

### Shredded memory foam (adjustable)

Small pieces of foam inside a zippered cover. You can add or remove fill to change the loft. Moldable, supportive and popular with side sleepers who like to customize. It can feel lumpy to some people, and many need a few minutes of fluffing.

### Solid memory foam

One block of foam that contours slowly to your head and neck. It keeps a consistent height and feels very stable. Some people find it warm, and you can't change the loft.

### Latex

Responsive and buoyant — it pushes back gently instead of letting you sink. Often described as cooler and more durable than many foams. Latex pillows can be heavier and pricier, and some people are sensitive to latex. See our deep dive on [memory foam vs. latex pillows](/guides/memory-foam-vs-latex-pillows/).

### Down and down alternative

Soft, light and very moldable. They feel luxurious, but most need a generous fill (or a firmer variety) to give side sleepers enough height, and they need regular fluffing.

### Contoured (cervical-shaped)

Shaped with a raised edge or a dip in the middle to cradle the neck and head. Many side sleepers like the defined shape, but it takes some getting used to and doesn't suit everyone.

## Step 5: Consider adjustability

If you're not sure what loft you need — or you share a bed and want to compare — **adjustable pillows** are one of the most forgiving choices. Look for:

- A zippered cover with access to the fill.
- Extra fill included, or a way to buy more.
- Removable inserts or layers (some foam and latex pillows offer stackable layers).

Make small changes — a handful of fill at a time — and give each version a few nights before you judge it.

## Step 6: Think about temperature and cover fabric

Side sleepers have a lot of face and ear contact with their pillow, so cover fabric matters. Breathable covers such as cotton, bamboo-derived viscose or Tencel lyocell often feel cooler than tightly woven polyester. Solid memory foam tends to hold more heat than shredded foam or latex. If you sleep warm, see our [bedroom comfort guide](/guides/how-to-make-your-bedroom-more-comfortable-for-sleep/) for more ways to keep your sleep space cool.

## Step 7: Check care and durability

A pillow you can keep clean is a pillow you'll enjoy longer. Before buying, check:

- **Is the cover removable and machine-washable?**
- **Can the inner pillow be washed?** Many foam and latex pillows can only be spot-cleaned.
- **Is a pillow protector recommended?** A zippered protector helps keep the pillow fresh.
- **What's the warranty?** Longer warranties can signal confidence in durability.

Most pillows eventually lose their shape. A common rule of thumb is to replace your pillow when it no longer springs back, stays flat after fluffing, or has become lumpy or discolored.

## Step 8: Set a realistic budget

You don't need the most expensive pillow to be comfortable — but very cheap pillows often flatten quickly, which is a particular problem for side sleepers who rely on loft.

- **Budget:** basic polyester fiber or simple foam pillows. Fine for testing what you like, but may flatten sooner.
- **Mid-range:** shredded foam, solid memory foam and down-alternative pillows with better covers and adjustable fill.
- **Premium:** natural latex, high-quality down, and pillows with organic materials or longer warranties.

Wherever you shop, prioritize **trial periods and return policies**. A pillow that's great on paper might not suit you, and many brands offer sleep trials for exactly that reason.

## Common mistakes side sleepers make

1. **Buying by appearance.** A puffy pillow can flatten fast under your head.
2. **Ignoring the mattress.** The same pillow can feel too high on a soft bed and too low on a firm one.
3. **Not giving it time.** It can take a week or two to adjust to a new pillow, especially a contoured or firm one.
4. **Keeping a pillow too long.** A flattened pillow quietly loses the loft you need.
5. **Copying someone else's choice.** Your partner's favorite pillow may not match your shoulder width.

## Frequently asked questions

### What pillow height is best for side sleepers?

There's no single best height. It depends on your shoulder width, body size and mattress firmness. Many side sleepers do well with a medium-high to high loft. The best test is whether your head and neck stay roughly level with your spine when you lie on your side.

### Is a firm or soft pillow better for side sleeping?

Side sleepers often prefer medium-firm to firm pillows because they hold their height. Soft pillows can work if they're full enough to keep their loft under the weight of your head.

### Should side sleepers use two pillows?

Some people stack pillows to get enough height, but two pillows can shift apart during the night. A single pillow with the right loft — or an adjustable pillow — usually gives more consistent support. Some side sleepers also like a separate pillow between their knees for comfort.

### How often should I replace my pillow?

It depends on the material and how well it's cared for. Replace it when it no longer bounces back, stays lumpy or flat after fluffing, or looks worn. Check the manufacturer's guidance and warranty.

### Can a pillow fix neck pain?

We can't make that claim, and you should be cautious of any product that does. A comfortable pillow may help you feel more relaxed, but if you have ongoing pain, please speak with a healthcare professional.
