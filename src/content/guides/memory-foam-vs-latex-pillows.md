Memory foam and latex are two of the most popular pillow materials for side sleepers. Both can hold a generous loft, both resist flattening better than many traditional fills, and both come in solid and shredded versions. Yet they feel surprisingly different once your head hits the pillow.

This guide compares them side by side — feel, support, temperature, durability, care, cost and who each one may suit — so you can choose the material that fits *your* preferences. As with all our guides, this comparison is research-based rather than the result of hands-on testing.

New to pillow shopping? Our pillar guide, [How to Choose a Pillow for Side Sleeping](/guides/how-to-choose-a-pillow-for-side-sleeping/), explains loft and firmness first.

## The short answer

- **Choose memory foam** if you like a slow, cradling, "sink-in" feel and value steady, consistent support.
- **Choose latex** if you prefer a bouncy, responsive feel, change positions often, or tend to sleep warm — and you don't have a latex sensitivity.

Both can work well for side sleepers. The deciding factors are usually feel, temperature and budget.

## What is memory foam?

Memory foam is a type of polyurethane foam engineered to respond to heat and pressure. When you lie down, it softens and slowly molds to the shape of your head and neck. When you lift your head, it gradually returns to its original shape.

Memory foam pillows come as:

- **Solid** blocks (rectangular, loaf or contoured shapes).
- **Shredded** pieces inside a zippered cover, which let you adjust loft.

Some manufacturers add gel, graphite, copper or other infusions, or use open-cell structures and ventilation holes, aiming to improve airflow.

## What is latex?

Latex foam is made from the sap of rubber trees (natural latex), from petroleum-based compounds (synthetic latex), or a blend of both. It's processed in one of two common ways:

- **Dunlop:** typically denser and firmer.
- **Talalay:** typically lighter, softer and more uniform in feel.

Like memory foam, latex pillows come in solid and shredded versions. Solid latex pillows often have small pinholes throughout, which are part of the manufacturing process and help with airflow.

## Side-by-side comparison

| Factor | Memory foam | Latex |
|---|---|---|
| Feel | Slow, contouring, "hug" | Springy, buoyant, "lift" |
| Response time | Slow to recover shape | Quick to bounce back |
| Support for side sleepers | Stable, even support | Firm, supportive push-back |
| Temperature | Can retain heat (varies by design) | Often sleeps cooler |
| Durability | Good; quality varies widely | Often very durable |
| Weight | Medium | Often heavier |
| Odor when new | Can have a temporary smell | Can have a mild natural scent |
| Allergy considerations | Generally suitable for most people | Avoid if you have a latex allergy |
| Typical price | Budget to premium | Mid-range to premium |

## Feel and support

This is the biggest difference — and the one that matters most.

**Memory foam** lets your head sink in gradually, creating a cradle. Many side sleepers enjoy this because it fills the curve of the neck and stays put. The downside is that slow response can make it feel like you're "stuck" in a dent when you roll over.

**Latex** supports you from below without much sinking. It feels lively and pushes back gently, so your head stays nearer the top of the pillow. Side sleepers who change positions often or dislike the sink-in sensation tend to prefer this. Some find it too bouncy at first.

Either way, **loft still matters more than material**. A latex pillow that's too low won't feel right, and neither will a memory foam pillow that's too tall. If you're unsure, a shredded version of either material lets you adjust.

## Temperature

Side sleepers rest their face and ear on the pillow, so heat can be noticeable.

- **Memory foam** has a reputation for trapping heat, especially in solid, dense designs. Shredded fill, open-cell foam, ventilation holes and breathable covers can help.
- **Latex** is generally considered more breathable thanks to its open structure and pinholes, and it doesn't soften with body heat in the same way.

If you often feel too warm at night, also look at your bedroom setup. Our guide on [making your bedroom more comfortable for sleep](/guides/how-to-make-your-bedroom-more-comfortable-for-sleep/) covers temperature, bedding and airflow.

## Durability

Both materials typically outlast basic polyester fiber pillows. Latex is often regarded as especially resilient, keeping its shape and bounce for a long time. Memory foam durability depends a lot on foam density and quality; lower-density foam may soften or develop a permanent dent sooner.

Check the manufacturer's warranty — it's a useful clue to how long a pillow is expected to last.

## Care and cleaning

Neither solid memory foam nor solid latex should usually go in the washing machine; water and agitation can damage the foam. Instead:

- Use a **removable, washable cover** and wash it regularly.
- Add a **zippered pillow protector** for extra protection.
- **Spot-clean** the core with mild soap and water if needed, then air-dry fully away from direct sunlight and heat.
- Some shredded pillows allow washing the inner liner — always follow the care label.

## Allergies and sensitivities

**Memory foam** is generally suitable for most people. Some notice a "new foam" smell when unboxing; airing the pillow out in a ventilated room usually helps.

**Latex** should be avoided by anyone with a latex allergy. If you're unsure, talk with a healthcare professional before buying. Many manufacturers also list third-party certifications for materials and emissions — look for this information on the product page.

## Cost

Memory foam pillows span the widest price range, from budget to premium. Latex pillows — especially those made from natural latex — typically sit in the mid-range to premium bracket because of material and processing costs. Look for sales, bundles and long trial periods to get the best value.

## Which should you choose?

### Memory foam may suit you if…

- You enjoy a cradling, sink-in feel.
- You tend to stay in one position for long stretches.
- You want lots of options at different price points.
- You don't usually sleep hot (or you choose a ventilated or shredded design).

### Latex may suit you if…

- You prefer responsive, bouncy support.
- You change positions frequently.
- You tend to sleep warm.
- Durability is a priority and your budget allows it.
- You don't have a latex allergy.

### Still unsure?

Consider a **shredded** version of either material so you can adjust the loft, and choose a retailer with a generous trial period. You can compare these options alongside down and contoured designs in [Best Pillows for Side Sleepers: What to Consider](/guides/best-pillows-for-side-sleepers/).

## Frequently asked questions

### Is latex or memory foam firmer?

It depends on the specific product. Latex usually *feels* firmer and more supportive because it pushes back, while memory foam feels softer on the surface as it molds to you. Both are available in a range of firmness levels.

### Which is better for side sleepers?

Neither is better for everyone. Both can provide the loft and support side sleepers typically look for. Choose based on the feel you prefer, how warm you sleep, and your budget.

### Does memory foam smell?

New memory foam can have a temporary odor from manufacturing. Letting the pillow air out in a well-ventilated room for a day or two usually reduces it.

### Can I wash a latex or memory foam pillow?

Usually not in a washing machine. Wash the removable cover, use a pillow protector and spot-clean the core. Always follow the manufacturer's care instructions.
