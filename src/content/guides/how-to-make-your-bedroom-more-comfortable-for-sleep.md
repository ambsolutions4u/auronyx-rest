The right pillow and mattress go a long way, but they're only part of the picture. Your bedroom itself — how dark, cool, quiet and inviting it feels — shapes how easily you relax at the end of the day.

The good news: most improvements are simple, inexpensive and easy to try one at a time. This guide covers light, temperature, noise, bedding, clutter and a wind-down routine, with a few extra notes for side sleepers. These are general comfort tips, not medical advice.

> **A quick note:** If you have persistent trouble falling or staying asleep, loud snoring, pauses in breathing, or you feel very tired during the day despite enough time in bed, please talk with a healthcare professional. Bedroom changes are about comfort; they aren't a substitute for medical care.

## 1. Control the light

Light is one of the strongest signals to your body about whether it's time to be awake or asleep. General sleep-hygiene advice from health organizations commonly recommends a **dark bedroom** at night and **bright light** during the day.

### Ideas to try

- **Blackout curtains or blinds** to block streetlights and early sunrise.
- **A comfortable sleep mask** — side sleepers may prefer a contoured or slim mask that doesn't press on the eyes or slip when your face meets the pillow.
- **Cover or remove small glowing lights** from chargers, TVs and power strips.
- **Use dim, warm lighting** in the evening, such as a bedside lamp with a warm bulb.
- **Keep a soft night light** in the hallway or bathroom if you get up at night, so you don't need bright overhead lights.

### Screens

Many people find it easier to unwind when they put phones, tablets and laptops away a while before bed. If you do use a screen, lowering the brightness and using a night mode setting may make the evening feel calmer.

## 2. Find a comfortable temperature

Most people sleep more comfortably in a room that feels **cool rather than warm**. There's no single perfect number — the right temperature depends on you, your bedding and your climate — so experiment and notice what helps you settle.

### Ideas to try

- **Adjust the thermostat** a little cooler in the evening if you can.
- **Use a fan** for airflow (bonus: it can double as background sound).
- **Layer your bedding** so you can add or remove a blanket during the night.
- **Choose breathable fabrics** like cotton, linen or lyocell for sheets and sleepwear.
- **Consider your pillow and topper materials.** Solid memory foam can feel warmer; latex, shredded foam and wool are often described as more breathable. Learn more in [Memory Foam vs. Latex Pillows](/guides/memory-foam-vs-latex-pillows/) and our [mattress topper guide](/guides/mattress-toppers-for-side-sleepers/).
- **Keep cold feet cozy** with socks or a light throw at the foot of the bed — warm feet help many people feel relaxed.

## 3. Reduce noise

Sudden or irregular sounds — traffic, neighbors, a partner's movements — can be distracting when you're trying to relax.

### Ideas to try

- **White noise or nature sounds** from a dedicated machine, a fan or an app to mask background noise.
- **Earplugs** made for sleeping. Side sleepers often prefer soft, low-profile earplugs that don't press uncomfortably against the pillow.
- **Soft furnishings** such as rugs, curtains and upholstered headboards absorb some sound.
- **Seal drafts** around doors and windows, which can also help with temperature.
- **Silence notifications** on your phone or use a "do not disturb" schedule.

## 4. Upgrade your bedding — starting with your pillow

Bedding is where side sleepers can often make the biggest difference in comfort.

### Your pillow

Side sleepers usually need a pillow with enough loft to keep the head level with the spine. If you're folding your pillow, stacking two, or tucking your arm underneath, it may be time to reassess. Start with [How to Choose a Pillow for Side Sleeping](/guides/how-to-choose-a-pillow-for-side-sleeping/), then compare options in [Best Pillows for Side Sleepers: What to Consider](/guides/best-pillows-for-side-sleepers/).

### Your mattress surface

If your shoulder and hip feel pressed into a hard surface, a softer comfort layer may feel better. Our [Mattress Toppers for Side Sleepers](/guides/mattress-toppers-for-side-sleepers/) guide explains when a topper may help and when a new mattress may be the better choice.

### Extra pillows

Many side sleepers like a **pillow between the knees** for comfort, or a **body pillow** to hug, which can keep the top arm and leg from rolling forward.

### Sheets and blankets

- Choose sheets you enjoy the feel of — crisp percale, smooth sateen, soft jersey or cool linen.
- Wash bedding regularly; fresh sheets simply feel nicer.
- Use pillow and mattress protectors to keep everything clean and extend its life.

## 5. Declutter and create a calm space

A tidy, uncluttered room can feel more restful. You don't need a full makeover — small changes add up.

- **Keep the bedroom mainly for sleep and relaxation** where possible, rather than work.
- **Clear the nightstand** down to a few essentials: a lamp, a glass of water, perhaps a book.
- **Choose calming colors** — soft blues, greens and warm neutrals tend to feel soothing.
- **Add something you love,** like a plant, a favorite photo or a pleasant (gentle) scent.
- **Hide cables and devices** so the room feels less like an office.

## 6. Build a relaxing wind-down routine

Your environment works best alongside a consistent routine. General sleep guidance often encourages keeping a **regular sleep and wake time**, including on weekends, as much as your schedule allows.

### Ideas for a calming evening

- Dim the lights and lower the volume in the hour before bed.
- Take a warm shower or bath.
- Read a paper book, listen to quiet music or try gentle stretching.
- Write tomorrow's to-do list so it's out of your head.
- Keep the routine short and repeatable — consistency matters more than perfection.

Many people also find it helps to be mindful of late-evening caffeine, heavy meals and alcohol. If you have questions about how these affect you personally, a healthcare professional is the best person to ask.

## 7. A quick bedroom comfort checklist

Use this list to spot easy wins:

- [ ] The room is dark at night (curtains, mask, no glowing lights).
- [ ] The temperature feels cool and comfortable.
- [ ] Noise is minimized or masked.
- [ ] Your pillow keeps your head level when lying on your side.
- [ ] Your mattress surface lets your shoulder and hip settle comfortably.
- [ ] Bedding is clean, breathable and pleasant to touch.
- [ ] The room feels tidy and calm.
- [ ] You have a simple, repeatable wind-down routine.

Try changing one or two things at a time and notice how you feel over a week or two.

## Frequently asked questions

### What's the best bedroom temperature for sleep?

It varies from person to person. Many people are most comfortable in a room that feels cool, with bedding they can adjust. Experiment with your thermostat, fans and layers to find what works for you.

### Is white noise good for sleep?

Many people find steady background sound helpful for masking sudden noises. Whether it helps you is personal — try a fan or a white noise machine at a low, comfortable volume.

### What else can side sleepers do to be more comfortable?

Make sure your pillow loft matches your shoulder width, consider a pillow between your knees, and check that your mattress surface isn't too firm for your shoulders and hips.

### When should I talk to a doctor about my sleep?

If sleep problems persist, affect your daytime life, or come with symptoms like loud snoring, gasping, or pauses in breathing, talk with a healthcare professional. They can help in ways a bedroom makeover can't.
